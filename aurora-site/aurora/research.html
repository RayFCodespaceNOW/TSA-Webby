Research questions

What we still want to know

The reason to go is not to prove what we already know. It is to answer questions that robots alone may not be able to settle.

Six questions for AURORA-1

How to read this site:

Fact checked against a published source

Concept an illustrative design idea

Assumption invented for this fictional mission

Open not yet answered by science

  1. Was Mars ever habitable?

    FactRobotic missions have found landforms that scientists interpret as shaped by liquid water long ago.

    OpenHow long water lasted, and whether conditions were gentle enough for life, is still being worked out.

  2. Is there life on Mars today, or was there ever?

    OpenThis is one of the biggest open questions in science. No life has been found on Mars, and nothing in this project claims otherwise.

    A crewed mission could examine samples directly, but any search must be designed to avoid mistaking Earth microbes for Martian ones.

  3. How can crews stay safe from radiation?

    OpenSpace radiation is a serious concern for long trips. Shields, storm shelters and shorter routes all help, and none solves the problem alone.

  4. Could a crew use what is already on Mars?

    FactMars’s atmosphere is about 95% carbon dioxide, which could in principle be a raw material.

    OpenWhether water ice is easy enough to reach and use at a landing site needs more study.

  5. How do we protect Mars from Earth?

    ConceptSpace agencies follow “planetary protection” rules that try to keep Earth microbes from contaminating other worlds. A human mission makes that much harder, because people carry microbes with them.

  6. What changes when help is far away?

    FactRadio signals take many minutes each way between Earth and Mars, so there can be no real-time chat.

    ConceptCrews would need more freedom to decide, plus clear plans for emergencies.

Significance

Why Mars matters to people your age

The first people to walk on Mars may be students today. The skills in this project, such as engineering, medicine, geology, communication and teamwork, are the same ones a real mission would need.

Many technologies built for space, from water recycling to compact medical tools, also help people on Earth.
