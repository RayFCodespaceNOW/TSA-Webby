Sources for facts

Facts are written in our own words. No text was copied from these pages.

  1. European Space Agency. “Facts about Mars.” Mars Express. esa.int/Our_Activities/Space_Science/Mars_Express/Facts_about_Mars.
    Used for: length of a sol (24 h 37 min), surface gravity (3.711 m/s²), atmosphere (95.32% carbon dioxide), diameter (6,794 km) and orbital period (687 Earth days). The “about 38% of Earth’s gravity” figure is our own calculation: 3.711 divided by 9.81.
  2. NASA Space Science Data Coordinated Archive (NSSDCA). “Mars Launch Windows.” nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/mars/marslaun.html.
    Used for: launch windows about every 26 months, and the fast trajectory of about 180 days described in NASA mission planning.

Statements without a listed source. General statements about radiation, weakened muscles and bones in weightlessness, thin Martian air during landing, weaker sunlight at Mars, planetary protection, and radio delay are widely known. The same goes for the relative orbit distances in the Overview drawing (about 0.4, 0.7, 1.0 and 1.5 times Earth’s distance from the Sun for Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars) and the shape of the transfer path. They have not yet been tied to a specific source on this page. The team still needs to add a citation for each one.

How the site is built

The site uses plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It uses no framework, no template and no theme, and it loads no outside libraries. NASA archive images load directly from NASA’s image servers; all other files are local. It collects no personal information.

AURORA is a fictional concept for education. It is not endorsed by NASA, ESA or any other agency.
