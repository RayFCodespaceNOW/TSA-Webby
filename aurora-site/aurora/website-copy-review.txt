Rewrite:
Stage 1 · Before launch
Rewrite:
Prepare and wait for the window
Rewrite:
Earth and Mars line up well for a trip only about once every 26 months, so missions must launch in a short window.
Rewrite:
Stage 2 · Outbound
Rewrite:
The long way out
Rewrite:
AURORA-1 can pick a faster or slower route. A NASA planning study described a fast trajectory of about 180 days.
Rewrite:
How crews stay healthy and motivated for months in microgravity is still being studied.
Rewrite:
Stage 3 · Arrival
Rewrite:
Enter orbit and descend
Rewrite:
A lander separates and carries the crew down. Mars’s thin atmosphere gives only some braking, which makes heavy landings hard.
Rewrite:
Stage 4 · Surface
Rewrite:
Work on Mars
Rewrite:
Crew collect samples, test equipment and check for water ice. Messages to and from Earth take many minutes, so the crew must decide many things themselves.
Rewrite:
Stage 5 · Return
Rewrite:
Wait, launch, and fly home
Rewrite:
The trip home also depends on where Earth and Mars are in their orbits, so crews may need to stay for a long time.
Rewrite:
Stage 6 · After landing
Rewrite:
Recovery and review
Rewrite:
Doctors check the crew, and engineers study what worked, so the next mission can be safer.

Rewrite:
1. Descent lander
Rewrite:
Carries the crew from orbit down to the surface.
Rewrite:
Mars’s atmosphere is thin, so it slows a spacecraft less than Earth’s does, yet it still heats the vehicle during entry. Landing something large and heavy is one of the hardest unsolved parts of a crewed mission.
Rewrite:
2. Crew habitat
Rewrite:
Where four people live, eat, exercise and work on the way to Mars.
Rewrite:
Long stays in weightlessness weaken muscles and bones, so daily exercise is a core part of the plan. This concept leaves out exact room sizes on purpose.
Rewrite:
3. Radiation shelter
Rewrite:
A small, shielded space where the crew can wait out solar storms.
Rewrite:
Radiation outside Earth’s protective magnetic field is a major health concern for long trips. How much shielding is enough is still being researched.
Rewrite:
4. Supply module
Rewrite:
Stores food, spare parts and recycling equipment for the whole trip.
Rewrite:
Everything launched from Earth is costly, so crews would recycle as much air and water as possible and carry spares for repairs they cannot order.
Rewrite:
5. Propulsion stage
Rewrite:
Pushes the spacecraft out of Earth orbit toward Mars.
Rewrite:
Space agencies have studied several engine types. AURORA does not choose one, because that decision needs real engineering analysis.
Rewrite:
6. Solar arrays
Rewrite:
Collect sunlight to make electricity for equipment, lights and life support.
Rewrite:
Mars is farther from the Sun than Earth is, so sunlight there is weaker. A real design would have to account for that, and for dust storms on the surface.
