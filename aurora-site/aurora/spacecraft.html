Technology

Connected Systems,
Distinct Roles.

A Mars mission crew depends on launch, entry and exit, life support, communication, navigation, and return capabilities. This page represents the roles those systems support in the AURORA concept.

The descriptions are fictional. They do not represent actual spacecraft designs.

Spacecraft systems

A rough sketch, not to scale or an engineering design.

1. Descent lander

Carries the crew from orbit down to the surface.

Mars’s atmosphere is thin, so it slows a spacecraft less than Earth’s does, yet it still heats the vehicle during entry. Landing something large and heavy is one of the hardest unsolved parts of a crewed mission.

2. Crew habitat

Where four people live, eat, exercise and work on the way to Mars.

Long stays in weightlessness weaken muscles and bones, so daily exercise is a core part of the plan. This concept leaves out exact room sizes on purpose.

3. Radiation shelter

A small, shielded space where the crew can wait out solar storms.

Radiation outside Earth’s protective magnetic field is a major health concern for long trips. How much shielding is enough is still being researched.

4. Supply module

Stores food, spare parts and recycling equipment for the whole trip.

Everything launched from Earth is costly, so crews would recycle as much air and water as possible and carry spares for repairs they cannot order.

5. Propulsion stage

Pushes the spacecraft out of Earth orbit toward Mars.

Space agencies have studied several engine types. AURORA does not choose one, because that decision needs real engineering analysis.

6. Solar arrays

Collect sunlight to make electricity for equipment, lights and life support.

Mars is farther from the Sun than Earth is, so sunlight there is weaker. A real design would have to account for that, and for dust storms on the surface.
