Crew roster

The four who would go

Real expeditions succeed because of the people as much as the machines. These crew members are fictional, and each one stands for a skill a Mars mission would need.

Meet the crew

These people are not real. The names, roles and questions were invented for this project. They are not real astronauts, and no real credentials or biographies are claimed.

Mission Commander

Ines Valdar

Keeps the plan, the schedule and the team on track, and makes the final call when the crew must choose.

Question they would ask
Messages from Earth take many minutes. When should the crew wait for advice, and when must they act?
Why this role
Chosen as a role that shows leadership under long-distance pressure.

FictionalCharacter created for this project.

Flight Engineer

Tomas Reyes

Watches the spacecraft’s power, air and water systems, and repairs things when they break.

Question they would ask
Which spare parts matter most, if you can only bring so many?
Why this role
Chosen to show how engineering and problem solving keep a crew alive.

FictionalCharacter created for this project.

Mission Scientist

Mei Lindqvist

Collects rock, soil and ice samples and decides what is worth studying on Mars.

Question they would ask
Was Mars ever a place where life could have started?
Why this role
Chosen to connect the trip to the science that makes it worth doing.

FictionalCharacter created for this project.

Medical and Systems Officer

Priya Nandakumar

Looks after the crew’s health, exercise and well-being, and helps keep life-support systems running.

Question they would ask
How do you keep four people healthy, rested and getting along for hundreds of days?
Why this role
Chosen to show that health and teamwork are as important as machines.

Why four?

Small teams, many skills

AssumptionAURORA-1 uses four people as a simple number to tell the story. A real crew size would be chosen after studying workload, safety and cost.

Whatever the size, each person would need a main job and a back-up skill, so that the loss of one person for a few days would not stop the mission.
