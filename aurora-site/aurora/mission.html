Mission dossier

AURORA-1: the plan

A fictional four-person expedition to Mars, built to show how a real mission would fit together.

Goals and limits

What AURORA-1 sets out to do

AssumptionLand four people on Mars, let them work there, and bring them home safely.

AssumptionStudy Mars’s rocks and ice to learn whether the planet was ever habitable.

AssumptionTest how people, machines and teamwork cope with long distance.

What this concept does not do

It is not an engineering design. It has no budget, no real launch date and no tested hardware. Numbers marked as assumptions were chosen to make the story clear, not because a real program uses them.

Mission timeline

From launch pad to home

Scroll down. The step nearest the middle of your screen lights up.

  1. Stage 1 · Before launch

    Prepare and wait for the window

    FactEarth and Mars line up well for a trip only about once every 26 months, so missions must launch in a short window.

  2. Stage 2 · Outbound

    The long way out

    AssumptionAURORA-1 can pick a faster or slower route. A NASA planning study described a fast trajectory of about 180 days.

    OpenHow crews stay healthy and motivated for months in microgravity is still being studied.

  3. Stage 3 · Arrival

    Enter orbit and descend

    ConceptA lander separates and carries the crew down. Mars’s thin atmosphere gives only some braking, which makes heavy landings hard.

  4. Stage 4 · Surface

    Work on Mars

    AssumptionCrew collect samples, test equipment and check for water ice. Messages to and from Earth take many minutes, so the crew must decide many things themselves.

  5. Stage 5 · Return

    Wait, launch, and fly home

    FactThe trip home also depends on where Earth and Mars are in their orbits, so crews may need to stay for a long time.

  6. Stage 6 · After landing

    Recovery and review

    ConceptDoctors check the crew, and engineers study what worked, so the next mission can be safer.
