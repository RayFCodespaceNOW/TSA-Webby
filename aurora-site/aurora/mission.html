Mission dossier

AURORA-1: the plan

A fictional four-person expedition to Mars, built to show how a real mission would fit together.

Goals and limits

What AURORA-1 sets out to do

Land four people on Mars, let them work there, and bring them home safely.

Study Mars’s rocks and ice to learn whether the planet was ever habitable.

Test how people, machines and teamwork cope with long distance.

What this project does not cover

This is not an engineering design. There is no budget, launch date or tested hardware. The route and schedule are examples, not plans for a real mission.

Mission timeline

From launch pad to home

  1. Stage 1 · Before launch

    Prepare and wait for the window

    Earth and Mars line up well for a trip only about once every 26 months, so missions must launch in a short window.

  2. Stage 2 · Outbound

    The long way out

    AURORA-1 can pick a faster or slower route. A NASA planning study described a fast trajectory of about 180 days.

    How crews stay healthy and motivated for months in microgravity is still being studied.

  3. Stage 3 · Arrival

    Enter orbit and descend

    A lander separates and carries the crew down. Mars’s thin atmosphere gives only some braking, which makes heavy landings hard.

  4. Stage 4 · Surface

    Work on Mars

    Crew collect samples, test equipment and check for water ice. Messages to and from Earth take many minutes, so the crew must decide many things themselves.

  5. Stage 5 · Return

    Wait, launch, and fly home

    The trip home also depends on where Earth and Mars are in their orbits, so crews may need to stay for a long time.

  6. Stage 6 · After landing

    Recovery and review

    Doctors check the crew, and engineers study what worked, so the next mission can be safer.
